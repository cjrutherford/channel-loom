## What was measured

The checker samples pixels from the decoded video approximately once per second and decodes a 13-bit source-time marker, not just the player clock. It rejects an adjacent backward change greater than one second within the same persisted broadcast identity, gaps between samples over four seconds, markers outside the scheduled range or more than 60 seconds behind/two seconds ahead, a marker unchanged for five seconds, and unexpected seeking, pause, or player error. It also requires overall marker progress of at least 3,595 seconds; observed progress was exactly 3,595. Thus a sampled ten-second backward marker jump would fail, but the sampling and one-second tolerance cannot rule out every brief event between samples, smaller replay, or marker decoding anomaly. No intentional seek is supplied by this test. The 4,200-second fixture and one persisted broadcast covering the run mean a normal fixture wrap or broadcast boundary is not expected during this interval; other real broadcasts or intentional seeks require separate interpretation.

In the retained 378 samples (elapsed 3,220.059–3,599.867 seconds), one adjacent marker went 3424 → 3423 → 3424 while player time and decoded frame counts advanced. This one-second decrease is allowed by the checker. A transient marker read or frame/encoding boundary is plausible, but the retained samples cannot establish its cause or prove an actual replay. It is not a ten-second regression.

The summary reports zero accumulated frame-counter stall seconds, 11 waiting/playing rebuffer episodes totaling 5.632 seconds, and zero counted network/HTTP errors, against a 10-second budget. A frame stall is only accumulated after the decoded frame counter has not changed for more than two seconds; zero does not prove absence of all shorter freezes. Rebuffer accounting uses media events after initial playback instrumentation is reset, and the 300-entry player event buffer can silently discard older events between samples. Request failures containing ERR_ABORTED are excluded; HTTP responses at least 400 are counted. hls.js internal reconnects are not instrumented. The final retained sample reports 107,728 total and 56 dropped video frames; dropped frames are a separate browser counter, not stall seconds. No media events appear in the retained sample window.

Its earliest retained warning, at 03:03:27.234849 UTC, is `private channel runtime failed: item boundary differs from published cursor`, about 2,409 seconds after sampling started. The immediately preceding lines show a private playlist and segment observed, and a segment prepared for scheduled publication. Subsequent warnings report supervisor and background playlist ensure failures with restart backoff. Across the retained tail there are 60 boundary/runtime warnings, 129 supervisor ensure warnings, and 185 background playlist ensure warnings; the final retained warning is at 03:23:14 UTC. These counts describe warning lines, not necessarily distinct root incidents. The tail does not prove the first failure occurred at 03:03:27, nor does it establish what happened before it.

Current source throws that boundary error when a supplied item's scheduled start differs from the publisher cursor by more than the configured tolerance. The recorded binary hash identifies the executable, but the run does not establish that this current source produced it. Even if it did, the warning alone does not reveal the two values, why they diverged, or whether these server failures caused any particular rebuffer. Browser heartbeats and the successful decoded-marker checks show playback continued through the interval; they do not establish uninterrupted publisher health or a causal connection to the 11 rebuffer episodes.

## Recommended next diagnostic

1. Preserve full timestamped server logs and detailed marker/player/network events on the next controlled run; capture boundary inputs (scheduled start, anchor, cursor, tolerance, broadcast identity and generation) at the publisher rejection, and correlate them with player waiting events and playlist/segment sequence. Record exact source diff and build identity. This will distinguish an actual publisher continuity defect from an incorrect boundary input or checker interpretation.
2. Inspect the publisher restart and item-boundary logic using those values before selecting a fix. Reproduce a suspected replay with retained decoded frames/markers and playlist timestamps, explicitly identifying seeks, broadcast boundaries and fixture wraps; do not infer content replay from player time alone.
3. Consider retaining lossless or bounded-on-disk evidence for the whole run and recording hls.js reconnects and event-buffer overflow, so an aggregate pass can be audited independently. No code or tests were run or changed for this analysis.
